Sector Value (SV) File from 6eBreeze — A Pilot's Reference

PAH FLT Issue 01 Rev 46 · Last verified 4 May 2026 · Reading time ~7 min

The Sector Value file is a monthly Excel export from 6eBreeze that gives, per flight number and per route and per departure time-slot, the canonical pay-relevant block time in minutes. It is the airline's pay-system reference — a rate card, not a list of flights you flew. Knowing what's in it (and what isn't) clears up most of the confusion around why your payslip totals can differ from a back-of-envelope calculation off your PCSR.

What the SV file is and where to get it

The SV file is a single-sheet Excel workbook (.xlsx) downloaded from 6eBreeze: Departments → OCC → Sector Pay. A typical month's file contains thousands of rows — every published flight × route × time-slot combination IndiGo operated in the month.

The file is published around the 20th–21st of the following month, after end-of-month operational reconciliation. So your March SV file becomes available roughly 20 April. If you're trying to verify your payslip before that date, the SV file you need may not exist yet — see "What if the SV file isn't available yet?" below.

The columns, one at a time

A row from the SV file looks like this:

FLTNBR | DEP | ARR | CREATEDDATETIME | Time_Slot | StartTime | EndTime | SectorValue | TotalFlight | ACTYPE
ColumnMeaning
FLTNBRNumeric flight number, no 6E prefix. 519 = 6E519, 2052 = 6E2052.
DEP3-letter IATA departure airport. May have a trailing space — trim before comparing.
ARR3-letter IATA arrival airport. Same trim caveat. DEP === ARR means an irregular operation (air-return).
CREATEDDATETIMEExcel serial date — when the row was added to the pay system. Useful for audit, not for matching.
Time_SlotUTC hour band as HH_HH, e.g. 17_18 = 17:00–17:59 UTC. Critical: this is UTC, not IST.
StartTimeSlot start as a fraction of a day (Excel time format). 0.7083 = 17:00 UTC.
EndTimeSlot end as a fraction of a day. 0.7493 = 17:59 UTC.
SectorValueThe pay-relevant block time in minutes. 149 means 2h 29m. This is the canonical block-time the pay system applies for this flight × route × time-slot combination.
TotalFlightHow many times this flight × route × time-slot combo operated in the source period — for context, not pay.
ACTYPEAircraft type code (320, 321 etc.). Useful for cross-checks but not used in standard pay lookups.

The Time_Slot trap — why your SV lookup fails when you assume IST

This is the single most common manual-calculation mistake. The SV file's Time_Slot column is keyed in UTC hour bands, not IST. To find the row matching one of your sectors, you have to convert your IST departure time to UTC first.

The conversion is constant year-round: UTC = IST − 5 hours 30 minutes. India does not observe daylight saving.

Worked conversion. A 04:40 IST departure is 04:40 − 05:30 = −00:50, which wraps to 23:10 UTC the previous calendar day. That falls in the 23_24 UTC slot, not the 4_5 slot you might have guessed. Pilots looking up early-morning IST departures consistently miss this and conclude the SV file "doesn't have my flight" when it does — under the previous day's UTC slot.

SV is not the same as scheduled block

This is the second most common mistake. A flight's SectorValue is computed by IndiGo's pay system using internal rules. It is related to, but not identical to, the scheduled block time you'd get from STA − STD.

A worked illustration: a Captain's January DHF sectors might total 790 minutes by SV but 835 minutes by STA−STD. The 45-minute difference matters — at the Captain deadhead rate of ₹4,000 per scheduled block hour that's ₹3,000 of allowance.

Per PAH §1.0, deadhead allowance is paid on scheduled block (STA − STD) from the published schedule, NOT on SectorValue. That is unambiguous. If you compute deadhead from SV minutes you will routinely under- or over-pay yourself by tens of minutes per month. More on the deadhead rule.

Where SV is and isn't used in PAH allowances

AllowancePAH §Uses SV?What it actually uses
Deadhead§1.0NoScheduled block (STA − STD) from the airline's published schedule.
Layover (TLPD)§2.0NoDuration of a 10h+ rest at a non-home station, between two duties.
Tail Swap§6.0NoSame-duty aircraft registration change between consecutive sectors.
Transit§7.0NoHalt duration between two operating sectors at the same station — measured against scheduled and actual times.
Night Flying§9.0YesSectorValue is used as the block-time anchor for computing how many minutes of the sector fall in the 00:00–06:00 IST night window.

SectorValue also drives Sector Pay itself — your per-tier compensation under PAH §11.0 — but that's a separate pay line on the payslip from the five allowances. This guide focuses on the allowances.

Worked example — looking up one sector

Captain G. — looking up a March sector

Captain G. operated 6E5037 DEL→JAI on 19 March with an actual departure of 04:32 IST. They want to find the matching row in the March SV file.

Step 1 — translate the flight number. 6E5037 → FLTNBR = 5037. Strip the 6E prefix.

Step 2 — compute the UTC departure hour. 04:32 IST − 05:30 = −00:58, which wraps to 23:02 UTC the previous day. The UTC hour is 23, so Time_Slot = 23_24.

Step 3 — match. Filter the SV file to FLTNBR=5037 AND DEP="DEL" AND ARR="JAI" AND Time_Slot="23_24". (Trim trailing whitespace from DEP and ARR.)

FLTNBR | DEP | ARR | Time_Slot | SectorValue | TotalFlight | ACTYPE 5037 | DEL | JAI | 23_24 | 50 | 81 | 320

The matching row gives SectorValue = 50 minutes — that's the pay-system block time for this flight when it departs in the 23:00–24:00 UTC slot (i.e. early-morning IST).

What this means for Captain G.'s pay:

  • Night flying uses this 50-minute SectorValue as the block anchor: the entire scheduled block (04:45 IST to 05:35 IST = 50 min) falls inside the 00:00–06:00 IST night window, so all 50 minutes count as night minutes. Pay = 50 × ₹2,000 / 60 = ₹1,667.
  • If this had been a DHF sector, deadhead pay would be calculated from the scheduled block (STA − STD) from the published schedule, not from this 50-minute SV.

What if the SV file for my month isn't available yet?

SV files are typically published around the 20th of the following month. If you're trying to verify a payslip earlier than that — or you're reconciling an older month and the file has been archived — the calculator will fall back to a recent prior month's SV file as an approximation.

Sector pay rates and time-slot patterns are stable month to month for IndiGo's domestic network. A January SV file generally maps acceptably to February or March operations, with small differences only on flights that changed schedule slot. The calculator surfaces an "approximation in use" banner when this fallback is active so you know the result isn't from the canonical source.

Common reasons your SV lookup fails

1. You used IST instead of UTC for the time slot

The most common mistake by far. Always convert to UTC by subtracting 5h30m from your IST departure before mapping to the slot. Early-morning IST departures (00:00–05:30 IST) wrap to the previous calendar day's UTC.

2. Trailing whitespace in DEP or ARR

The SV file routinely stores "DEL " with a trailing space. "DEL " === "DEL" is false. Always .trim() when comparing programmatically; in Excel, use =TRIM(B2) to clean the column first.

3. The flight number was prefixed with 6E

SV stores FLTNBR as the bare integer. 6E519 in your PCSR = 519 in SV. Strip the prefix.

4. Same flight number, different routes

IndiGo reuses flight numbers across the day. 6E6045 may operate BOM→DEL in the morning and DEL→GAU in the afternoon — both rows exist in SV under FLTNBR=6045 with different DEP/ARR. Always match on flight number AND dep AND arr, not just flight number.

5. The flight isn't in this month's SV file because it operated in a different month

The SV file lists flights operated in the file's month. A sector operated on the last day of February will be in the February SV file, not March. If you're verifying a layover that spans the month boundary, the outbound and return sectors may live in different SV files.

How CrewAllowance.com uses your SV file

The calculator parses your uploaded SV file and uses it for one specific calculation: Night Flying allowance under PAH §9.0. For each sector you operated, it strips the 6E prefix from the PCSR flight number, converts your IST departure to a UTC time-slot, and matches against the SV row by (FLTNBR, DEP, ARR, Time_Slot). The matched SectorValue is then used as the block-time anchor for computing night minutes.

The other four allowances — Deadhead, Layover, Tail Swap, Transit — don't use the SV file. They run off PCSR data plus a flight-status data provider for scheduled/actual times and aircraft registrations.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Sector Value (SV) file?

The Sector Value file is a monthly Excel export from 6eBreeze (Departments → OCC → Sector Pay) that records, per flight number and per route and per departure time-slot, the canonical pay-relevant block time in minutes — the SectorValue. It is the airline's pay-system reference for what each flight 'is worth' as a pay metric. It is a rate card, not a list of flights you flew.

Where do I download the SV file from?

From 6eBreeze. Sign in, navigate to Departments → OCC → Sector Pay, and download the latest month's Excel (.xlsx) export. The SV file for a given month is typically published around the 20th–21st of the following month, after end-of-month operational reconciliation.

What's the difference between SV and scheduled block (STA−STD)?

Scheduled block is the published timetable: STA minus STD for a specific operation on a specific date. Sector Value is the pay-system reference for that flight number on that route in that time-slot — and it can differ from STA−STD by a few minutes either way. SV reflects pay-system rules including scheduled-block adjustments. The two are related but not identical, which is why deadhead allowance under PAH §1.0 uses scheduled block from the published schedule, not SV.

Why is Time_Slot in UTC and not IST?

The SV file is generated by IndiGo's pay system which standardises on UTC across the global network. Each row is keyed by a UTC hour band (e.g. 17_18 means 17:00–17:59 UTC). To look up your sector you need to convert your IST departure time to UTC by subtracting 5 hours 30 minutes. A 04:40 IST departure is 23:10 UTC the previous day, which falls in the 23_24 slot. This UTC keying trips up many manual calculations.

Which PAH allowances actually use the SV file?

Only Night Flying allowance under PAH §9.0 uses SectorValue as the canonical block-time anchor. Deadhead (§1.0) uses scheduled block STA−STD, not SV. Layover (§2.0), Tail Swap (§6.0), and Transit (§7.0) don't use SV at all — they rely on duty boundaries, aircraft registrations, and halt durations from the schedule. Sector Pay itself is a separate per-tier compensation under §11.0 that uses SectorValue, but that's a different pay line from the five allowances.

Why does my deadhead allowance differ from the SV total?

Because deadhead pays on scheduled block (STA − STD), not on SectorValue. Across a month with several DHF sectors the two totals can differ by tens of minutes, which translates to several hundred rupees. PAH §1.0 is unambiguous: scheduled block is the basis. If the SV minutes for a DHF sector look low, check it against STA − STD from the published schedule for the date of operation.

What does FLTNBR look like in the SV file?

FLTNBR is stored as an integer without the 6E prefix. So flight 6E519 is stored as 519, 6E2052 as 2052, 6E6045 as 6045. When matching against your PCSR (which shows 6E-prefixed flight numbers), strip the prefix and compare numerically. SV also doesn't carry letter suffixes — the air-return code 6805A appears as FLTNBR=6805 with DEP=ARR=BLR; the suffix and the round-trip pattern are inferred from DEP=ARR rather than from the flight number itself.

Why does the SV file sometimes have BLR-BLR or DEL-DEL rows?

Same departure and arrival airport in the SV file marks an irregular operation — most commonly an air-return (Ground-Turn-Back) where the aircraft took off, encountered a problem, and returned to the originating airport. The SectorValue for these rows reflects the actual block time of the abort, typically 30–90 minutes. The pilot's eventual successful flight to the original destination appears as a separate normal row with the original DEP and ARR.

What if the SV file for my month isn't available yet?

SV files are typically published around the 20th of the following month. If you're trying to verify a payslip earlier than that, the calculator can fall back to the previous 1–3 months' SV files as an approximation. Sector pay rates and time-slot patterns are stable month-to-month, so a January SV file usually maps acceptably to February or March operations. The calculator surfaces a banner when an approximation is in use so you know the result isn't from the canonical source.
