What Is a PCSR? IndiGo's Personal Crew Schedule Report Explained

PAH FLT Issue 01 Rev 46 · Last verified 4 May 2026 · Reading time ~7 min

The PCSR — Personal Crew Schedule Report — is IndiGo's end-of-month record of every flight you operated, every deadhead you took, every layover you stayed for, and every statistic your payslip is calculated from. It is the single document that ties together your roster, your hotel arrangements, your transfers, and the underlying data for all five PAH allowances. Knowing how to read each section turns the PCSR from a dense PDF into something you can verify line-by-line against your monthly pay.

What is in a PCSR

A PCSR is a multi-page PDF generated by IndiGo's crew-management system (AIMS) and made available through eCrew. The structure is consistent across pilots and months. A typical PCSR has four to seven pages depending on duty volume, broadly organised as follows:

  1. Page 1 — Calendar grid: a wide table with one column per calendar day in the month and stacked cells for the sectors, codes and times flown that day. Page 1 also carries the pilot's identity bar and the month's statistics block.
  2. Other Crew pages: for each operating day, the full crew roster — captain, first officer, cabin crew, plus any deadheading crew — annotated with their role on each sector.
  3. Hotel Info: the hotels arranged for any layover stays, with check-in and check-out dates and hotel codes.
  4. Transfer Info: ground transport between airports and hotels (and back) for layovers requiring transfers.
  5. Pax Transfer: any pilot-as-passenger flights arranged by IndiGo (commercial repositioning).
  6. Memos: a free-text section for assignment-change notices, acknowledgements, or special instructions issued during the month.

Pages 2 onwards repeat the report header on every page. The calendar grid is unique to page 1; everything else is supporting detail.

Page 1 — the calendar grid

This is the page you will look at most often. It is a single very wide table with one column per day of the month and a header row carrying the date and day-of-week. Below the header, each column stacks the sectors flown on that day in chronological order from top to bottom.

The pilot identity bar

A blue band near the top reads something like 16612 LASTNAME, FIRSTNAME DEL,CP,320. The four pieces are: your employee ID, your name as registered with IndiGo, your home base (3-letter IATA), your rank code (CP = Captain, FO = First Officer, etc.), and your fleet (320 = A320 family).

The date range

Just below the title is the period the report covers, e.g. 01/01/2026 - 31/01/2026 (All times in Local Station). The "Local Station" qualifier matters: every time printed in the body of the report is in the local time of the airport where the event happened (so a CCU departure is in IST; a SIN departure would be in SGT). For all-domestic months this is simply IST throughout.

How a sector cell is structured

Inside each calendar cell, a single sector is rendered top-to-bottom in this order:

  1. An A-prefixed actual time (the ATD — actual takeoff). Example: A07:42.
  2. The flight number, e.g. 2312 (the "6E" prefix is implied; the full number is 6E2312).
  3. The departure airport as a 3-letter IATA code, optionally prefixed with an asterisk if you were deadheading forward on this leg. Example: *BOM.
  4. The arrival airport, also 3-letter IATA. Example: DEL.
  5. An A-prefixed actual time (the ATA — actual arrival). Example: A09:46.
  6. The aircraft type in square brackets. Example: [320] for an A320, [321] for an A321.

If a sector continues into the next day (overnight flight, or arrival after midnight), a down-arrow appears at the bottom of the cell and the arrival side is shown at the top of the next column.

Indicator codes

A sector flown for a specific operational reason carries a short tag in parentheses immediately after the flight number, for example (L) or (C). The most common:

CodeMeaning
(T)Recency / SLF (Supervised Line Flying)
(R)Route check
(L)LHS — Left Hand Seat training
(W)Weather diversion
(C)Annual check / Operator Proficiency Check

Indicator codes don't change the allowance calculation but are useful when reconciling unusual entries — for example, a check sector that pays differently from a routine line sector under a separate Sector Pay schedule.

The statistics block

At the bottom of page 1, a horizontal strip carries five summary numbers for the month: Block Hours, Duty Hours, Dead Head, Flights, Landings. These are convenient totals but allowances are computed per sector, not from these aggregates. Landings can be one or two more than Flights when a duty includes an air-return or a non-revenue ferry.

The Other Crew section — the authoritative source for DHF/DHT

Pages 2 onwards carry, for each operating day in the month, the full crew complement on each sector. Lines look like:

06/01/2026 6045 CP - PIC - 16612 - LASTNAME, FIRSTNAME FO - 42537 - … LD - 13878 - … … 06/01/2026 519 CP - DHF - 16612 - LASTNAME, FIRSTNAME …

For each sector the entry against your employee number tells you exactly what role you filled:

  • PIC — Pilot In Command (you operated the sector)
  • FO — First Officer
  • DHF — Deadhead-Forward (positioning to operate something)
  • DHT — Deadhead-Terminating (positioning back, often after a swap)
Why this matters more than the asterisk. The asterisk on the calendar-grid departure airport is a visual shorthand for DHF and is occasionally missing or wrong. The Other Crew entry is what payroll uses to classify the sector. When the two disagree, the Other Crew section wins. Deadhead allowance (PAH §1.0) depends on this classification.

Hotel Info, Transfer Info, Pax Transfer

These sections list IndiGo-arranged accommodation, ground transport, and any commercial repositioning the airline booked for you. They are not used by the calculator for layover allowance under PAH §2.0 — that calculation runs purely off the page-1 schedule (a 10h+ stay at a non-home station between two duties qualifies regardless of whether IndiGo booked the hotel). But they are a useful audit trail when reconciling a layover line on your payslip back against an actual hotel night.

The Hotel Info table includes the hotel code (e.g. WESTIN_BOM, NOVOTELHYD, TAJAIRPORT_BLR) and the date range. Hotel codes follow the pattern BRAND_CITY in most cases.

Worked example — reading three days of a fictional pilot's PCSR

Captain F. — March, DEL base

Suppose Captain F.'s PCSR shows the following three columns, top-to-bottom in each cell:

DAY 8 DAY 9 DAY 10 ———————————— ———————————— ———————————— A14:30 (off) A16:37 6403 802 DEL BOM BLR AMD A17:17 A18:07 [320] [320] A18:42 A18:44 6404 803 BLR AMD DEL BOM A21:30 A20:12 [320] [321] ↓

What this tells you:

  • Day 8: Two sectors operated. DEL→BLR (167 minutes scheduled), then BLR→DEL (168 minutes). Both on a 320. Both actual times prefixed with A — these flew on the day. No asterisk on either departure airport, and the Other Crew section would show CP - PIC - … for both — Captain F. operated both legs.
  • Day 9: An off-day. No sectors, no asterisks. Captain F. is at home base (DEL) overnight.
  • Day 10: A round-trip BOM→AMD→BOM. The aircraft type changed mid-day from 320 to 321 — a tail swap (different physical aircraft). The down-arrow at the bottom of day 10's last cell signals the duty continued past midnight; the corresponding ATA would be at the top of day 11's column.

What this is worth for allowances (Captain rates, PAH §11.0 effective 1 January 2026):

  • Two operating sectors on day 8, two more on day 10 → no DHF, so no deadhead allowance.
  • The BOM→AMD→BOM round-trip on day 10 had a tail swap → ₹1,500 tail-swap allowance.
  • If Captain F. spent the night of day 7 at a non-home station before the day-8 duty, that's a TLPD layover. Day 9 at home doesn't pay TLPD.

End-of-month vs mid-month: which version do I use?

Pilots can request a PCSR at any point in the month. Two important versions:

  • Mid-month / "Schedule Report": a forecast. Future sectors haven't operated yet, so they show only scheduled times, no A prefix. Hotel Info and Transfer Info pages may be missing entirely.
  • End-of-month PCSR: the actuals. Every sector has an A-prefixed time pair. Hotel and Transfer sections are populated. This is the version your payslip is calculated from.

For allowance verification, always use the end-of-month version. It typically becomes available on the 1st of the following month. The CrewAllowance.com calculator rejects mid-month PCSRs to prevent confusing partial calculations.

Common things that confuse first-time PCSR readers

1. The asterisk and the Other Crew entry sometimes disagree

The asterisk is a visual shorthand; the Other Crew entry is the source of truth. If the asterisk is missing but Other Crew shows DHF, the sector pays as DHF. The asterisk is useful for spotting deadheads at a glance but not for verifying pay.

2. "All times in Local Station" surprises pilots on international duties

A SIN→BOM sector will show its departure time in Singapore time, not IST. The PAH allowances for Indian sectors are evaluated in IST (UTC+5:30) per the Pilot Allowance Handbook, so the calculator translates non-Indian local times automatically. If you're computing manually, remember to convert.

3. Letter suffixes on flight numbers

A flight number with a single trailing letter — e.g. 6805A — denotes an irregular operation, most often an air-return (Ground-Turn-Back). The sector is real and may have its own block time and aircraft, even though the destination ends up being the same as the origin. The PCSR will then carry a separate entry for the eventual successful operation under the unsuffixed flight number.

4. Block Hours in the stats block ≠ sum of sector times

Block Hours in the stats block is computed per IndiGo's internal rules (which include some adjustments for diversions, technical stops, and similar). For allowance verification you should use the per-sector scheduled block (STA − STD) for each operating day, not the headline total.

Frequently asked questions

What does PCSR stand for?

PCSR stands for Personal Crew Schedule Report. It is IndiGo's end-of-month record showing every sector you operated, every deadhead, every layover, every hotel and transfer arrangement, and a statistics block summarising your monthly block hours, duty hours and landings.

Where do I download my PCSR from?

From eCrew. Sign in, navigate to Reports, and select Personal Crew Schedule Report. You can request a PDF for any month including the current one, but the end-of-month version (typically available on the 1st of the following month) is the version your payslip is calculated from.

What's the difference between an end-of-month PCSR and a mid-month one?

A mid-month PCSR is a forecast — it shows the schedule as currently planned, including upcoming sectors that haven't operated yet, with scheduled-only times. An end-of-month PCSR is the actuals — every sector has actual times, every layover has real check-in/check-out, and the statistics block reflects what was actually flown. Always use the end-of-month version for allowance verification.

How do I read the calendar grid on page 1?

Each column is one calendar day; each cell stacks the day's sectors top-to-bottom. Within a cell, you'll see the actual takeoff time (A-prefixed), the flight number, the departure airport (with an asterisk if you were deadheading), the arrival airport, the actual arrival time (A-prefixed), and the aircraft type in square brackets. A down-arrow means the sector continues into the next column — typical for an overnight flight.

What is the Other Crew section and why does it matter?

The Other Crew section lists every crew member operating each sector and their role on that flight. Lines like CP - DHF - 16612 - LASTNAME, FIRSTNAME identify whether a pilot was deadheading forward (DHF) or terminating (DHT). This is the authoritative source for DHF/DHT classification — payroll uses it, not the asterisk in the calendar grid.

Why are some times prefixed with A?

The A prefix means actual. A22:33 is the actual takeoff or landing time. Times without an A prefix are scheduled. PCSRs print actuals wherever they exist (i.e. for sectors that have already operated). For DHF sectors, only scheduled times may appear because there is no operating-pilot ATD/ATA recorded.

What are the Hotel Info and Transfer Info sections used for?

Hotel Info lists each layover hotel and the dates of stay. Transfer Info lists ground transport between airport and hotel. They are useful for verifying which layovers involved an IndiGo-arranged hotel, but allowance calculation under PAH §2.0 is per duty boundary, not per hotel — a 10h+ stay at a non-home station between two duties qualifies for TLPD whether or not IndiGo booked the hotel.

What does the statistics block at the bottom of page 1 mean?

It summarises the month: Block Hours (total scheduled block flown), Duty Hours (total duty time including report and debrief), Dead Head (block hours flown as DHF), Flights (total sectors operated), Landings (total landings — can differ from flights when a duty includes a non-revenue ferry or air-return). Useful headline numbers, but allowances are calculated per sector.

What are the indicator codes like (T), (R), (L), (W), (C)?

Line-trainer or special-status flags appended to a sector. (T) = Recency / SLF, (R) = Route check, (L) = LHS training, (W) = Weather diversion, (C) = Annual / Operator Proficiency Check. They mark sectors flown for a specific operational purpose. They don't change allowance calculations but are useful context.

What is an air-return, and how is it shown in the PCSR?

An air-return (or Ground-Turn-Back, GTB) happens when a flight takes off and returns to the departure airport without reaching its destination — typically a technical issue or weather. The PCSR encodes this with a letter suffix on the flight number (e.g. 6805A), an actual takeoff time, and an actual landing time at the same airport. The pilot's eventual successful flight to the original destination — under the same flight number, no suffix — follows. Both sectors appear in the PCSR; allowances around the abort can be subtle and depend on whether an aircraft swap occurred.
